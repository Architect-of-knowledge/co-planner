# Better improvement suggestions

Start from the user's outcome and the observed friction. Generate several candidates internally; present the few worth deciding on. No quota, fabricated scores, or obligation to propose a new feature.

Useful lenses:
- Remove a step, a duplicate concept, an unnecessary choice or an entire maintenance burden.
- Make the first/default state useful; improve recovery from empty, error or interrupted states.
- Reuse an existing contract, signal or capability across relevant components.
- Join one component's output to another's missing input: A + B → a new user outcome.
- Turn existing feedback or measurement into a useful action.
- Address a demonstrated accessibility, reliability, performance or cost constraint.

For each substantial candidate, communicate concisely:
1. **Outcome:** who benefits and what becomes easier or possible.
2. **Basis:** concrete evidence or an explicitly labeled hypothesis; include paths/symbols when relevant.
3. **Tradeoff:** rough effort, dependencies, downside and what it displaces.
4. **Alternative:** the smallest useful change, including doing nothing when reasonable.
5. **Validation:** how to check the benefit or cheaply test the uncertain part.

Rank by relevance, benefit, confidence and cost. Keep A–D grades compatible with existing insights, but explain the grade: A = strong evidence and favorable cost; B = promising with a material caveat; C = weak fit or uncertain benefit; D = not worth pursuing under current constraints. Grades are judgments, not measured scores.

For third knowledge, confirm both capabilities exist and identify the joining contract. A clever pairing without a useful outcome is not a recommendation. Avoid exhaustive component-pair comparisons; follow plausible connections from the relevant map.

Challenge the leading candidate: what assumption would make it wrong? Does it conflict with accepted constraints or valuable exceptions? Could simplification achieve most of the benefit?

Record disposition and why. A budget deferral is not a dislike. Do not re-propose a rejected insight unless new evidence or changed constraints address its rejection. Keep uncertain preferences marked inferred until confirmed. Product comparisons require current sources when relied on; they are optional, not a research ritual.
